  • Mellatur (l_57)
    Coordinates 10.856212086812356, 79.23868178890949 View on map
    Place Types

    Settlement
    Notes
    During the Nāyaka dynasty, Acyutaparāya (1577-1614) gifted Mellatur village to 500+ brahmin actors families and scholars to preserve and continue the tradition of the Bhagavatamela - a regional form of theatre in Telugu blending classical dance and Carnatic music performed by male brahmin actors.
    Every year, on Narasimha Jayanti, the Bhagavatamela festival takes place for ten days in the agrahāra street facing the Varadaraja temple. The actors face the temple while the audience sits in the street.

  • Tippirajapuram (l_63)
    Coordinates 10.9084196, 79.4033356 View on map
    Place Types
    Settlement
    Notes
    Nicely preserved agrahāra with two temples: Vaiṣṇava (Varadarāja, with Venugopala during festivals) situated at the end of South street; and Śaiva (Viracolesvara).
    One of the eighteen Vathima villages inhabited mostly by smārta brahmins. Structured as a square formed of North, South, East, and West streets; a short extension of South Street, Sannidhi street, leads to the Vaiṣṇava temple in the south-west corner. South street is more orthodox and used to be composed exclusively of brahmins; the North street, by contrast, includes non-brahmin households. This difference between the two main streets of the agrahāra is reflected in their different materials: sand for South street, and tar for North street. See: Fuller, C. J. and Narasimhan, Haripriya. ‘The agraharam: The Transformation of Social Space and Brahman Status in Tamilnadu during the Colonial and Postcolonial periods.’ In Ritual, Caste, and Religion in Colonial South India, edited by Michael Bergunder, Heiko Freise and Ulrike Schröder, pp. 219-37. Halle: Neue Hallesche Berichte, 2010, pp. 224ff.
